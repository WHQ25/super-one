Voice is easy to start and hard to make legible afterwards. Most of this work is the afterwards.

### One ordered transcript

Voice turns and delegated Codex work render in a single ordered transcript rather than two parallel streams the reader has to interleave. Transcript items are stamped with a start time on arrival, because the realtime protocol does not carry timestamps of its own — without local stamping, ordering a voice conversation against tool work is guesswork.

### A vertical timeline

The voice transcript renders as a vertical timeline, so a long call reads as a sequence with duration rather than a wall of alternating bubbles.

### Know when it is listening

A ready cue tells you the session is live before you start talking, and live surfaces show state during the call. Prompt injection placeholders let you seed the conversation without speaking it.

### It persists

Realtime voice sessions are stored locally and stay deletable and titled like any other session — a call is a session, not a transient overlay.

### Side chat

Alongside it, side chat opens an ephemeral fork of the live conversation: ask something adjacent without derailing the turn in progress, and let it disappear when you are done.
