## What it does

The relay routes bytes between your desktop and your phone without being able to read them. Session contents are encrypted before they leave the desktop and decrypted on the device.

## How it works

Pairing establishes a master secret that only the two ends hold. From it, HKDF-SHA256 derives two separate keys:

- A **channel key**, used to compute the room id (SHA-256 of the channel key, truncated) and to sign HMAC auth tokens over `role:timestamp`.
- An **AES-GCM key**, used to encrypt every payload. Each message carries a fresh random 12-byte IV prepended to its ciphertext.

Files use a chunked envelope of the same construction: a versioned header, then 4 MB chunks each sealed under GCM with its own IV and tag, so a large transfer streams without ever holding the whole file in memory in plaintext.

## What the relay can see

Routing metadata: which room, how large, when. Not the contents. The room id is derived from the channel key rather than being a name you choose, so it does not leak the project or session it belongs to either.

## Why two derived keys instead of one

The key that proves who you are and the key that hides what you said should not be the same key. Deriving both from one secret keeps pairing simple; keeping them separate means a token that must be transmitted to authenticate does not double as the key that protects the payload.

## FAQ

**Does self-hosting the relay change this?** No — the relay is not trusted in either case, which is exactly why self-hosting is optional rather than necessary.
