## What it does

Fork copies a session at any past message and continues in a new one, leaving the original untouched.

## Why fork instead of rewind

Rewind moves a session back and discards what came after. Fork keeps both: the original stays as it was, and the copy continues from the chosen point. Use rewind when the later turns are simply wrong; use fork when they might be right and you want to try something else in parallel.

## How it works

- Pick any past message as the fork point. Everything up to it is carried over.
- The fork is a full local session — its own title, its own status, its own place in the sidebar.
- Both sessions run independently from that point on, including on different engines if you want to compare how two of them handle the same setup.

## FAQ

**Is forking supported on every engine?** The capability differs by harness and the app declares it as data rather than assuming — Claude forks via the SDK, Codex via a thread fork at a turn id, and the others through their own equivalents where they have one.
